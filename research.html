Job market paper

JMPThe Changing Timing of Work: Male Labor Supply Across Cohorts

Draft coming soon

Abstract

Non-college men in the US born 1955–1970 (the 1960 cohort) work less at prime ages and more at older ages than those born 1930–1945 (the 1930 cohort). I estimate a life-cycle model of labor supply, saving, and pension and Social Security decisions using PSID data for the 1930 cohort. The model incorporates health, wage, and survival risk, alongside cohort differences in pensions, marriage and spouse income, Social Security, and disability insurance. Holding preferences fixed at their 1930 cohort estimates, the model reproduces roughly 65–80 percent of the observed participation differences across age groups when given the 1960 cohort’s environment. Changes in marriage and spouse income drive most of the model’s prime-age decline. Wider disability insurance access also reduces participation among men with work limitations. At older ages, the change in wage profiles drives the participation increase, while the decline in defined-benefit pensions and Social Security reforms further raise participation.
